## Step 1 — Verify the enactment status before treating a bill as law

### Purpose

Do not assume that HB 2562, HB 2563, or HB 2564 became law merely because:

- They appear in a legislative database.
- A policy document refers to them.
- A health-plan name suggests that the legislation exists.
- A summary describes them as enacted.
- A bill number appears in a secondary source.

Each bill should first be classified as one of the following:

- Introduced
- Referred to committee
- Reported from committee
- Passed one chamber
- Passed both chambers
- Enrolled
- Signed into law
- Became law without signature
- Vetoed
- Withdrawn
- Failed or expired
- Status unresolved

Only the appropriate final categories should support a statement such as “Puerto Rico law requires…” or “the statute imposes…”

### Recommended workflow

For each bill, capture the following fields:

```text
jurisdiction
bill_number
session
short_title
official_title
subject
introduced_date
last_action_date
last_action
enactment_status
governor_action
act_number
effective_date
official_source_url
retrieved_at
verification_notes
```

The critical distinction is between a **bill identifier** and an **enacted law identifier**. A bill number is not automatically a statute. If enacted, it should generally be traceable to an act number, signed-law record, codified provision, or official session-law publication.

### What to verify

For HB 2562, HB 2563, and HB 2564:

1. Confirm the legislative session.
2. Confirm that the numbers refer to the relevant Puerto Rico measures rather than another jurisdiction or session.
3. Read the official status history.
4. Check whether the measure passed both chambers.
5. Check for executive approval, veto, or automatic enactment.
6. Locate the resulting act number if the bill became law.
7. Confirm the effective date.
8. Determine whether the relevant provision was later amended, repealed, delayed, or superseded.
9. Record the exact provision relevant to outpatient coinsurance, pharmacy deductibles, or annual cost-sharing caps.
10. Mark the result as `unconfirmed` if any essential link is missing.

### Safer wording

Use wording that matches the evidence:

- **Verified enactment:** “Act No. ___, enacted on ___, provides…”
- **Pending bill:** “HB ___, introduced on ___, would provide…”
- **Unresolved:** “The measure’s enactment status could not be confirmed from the available official record.”
- **Secondary-source-only:** “A secondary source describes HB ___ as addressing ___; official enactment was not independently confirmed.”

Avoid:

> Puerto Rico law caps outpatient coinsurance under HB 2562.

Prefer:

> HB 2562 is cited in the working materials as relating to outpatient cost sharing, but its enactment status and operative text must be confirmed before it is treated as binding law.

### Acceptance criteria

Step 1 is complete only when every measure has:

- An official status record.
- A status value.
- An official source.
- An act number, if enacted.
- An effective date, if enacted.
- A quoted or pinpointed operative provision.
- A clear distinction between proposed and enacted language.

A useful machine-readable status object would look like this:

```json
{
  "bill_number": "HB 2562",
  "jurisdiction": "Puerto Rico",
  "session": null,
  "enactment_status": "unconfirmed",
  "act_number": null,
  "effective_date": null,
  "official_source_url": null,
  "verified": false,
  "notes": "Do not cite as operative law until official status and text are located."
}
```

## Step 4 — Verify the legal framework instead of searching for nonexistent code sections

### Purpose

The instruction not to hunt for the “full text” of §28-725 or §29-8719 is important because an apparently precise citation can be misleading in several ways:

- The section number may not exist.
- The number may belong to a different title.
- The reference may be to a bill section rather than codified law.
- A section may have been renumbered.
- The source may have generated a citation from a template.
- The citation may combine a chapter number and section number incorrectly.
- The provision may exist only in a regulation, administrative rule, or session law.

Repeatedly searching for nonexistent text risks converting a bad citation into a false legal conclusion.

### Replace the search objective

Instead of asking:

> Where is the full text of §28-725 or §29-8719?

Ask:

> What official provisions govern network adequacy, disclosure, provider access, and related insurance-plan obligations, and can the claimed section numbers be mapped to those provisions?

That creates two separate research tracks:

1. **Positive verification:** locate the actual governing law.
2. **Negative verification:** document that the cited sections could not be confirmed as valid statutory references.

### Verify Act 68 of 1998 and Act 2 of 2023 separately

For each act, record:

```text
act_number
enactment_date
title_or_subject
official_session_law_url
codified_title
codified_sections
relevant_topic
amendments
repeals
effective_date
quoted_language
```

Do not treat “Act 68 of 1998” or “Act 2 of 2023” as sufficient by themselves. The research record should identify:

- What the act actually enacted.
- Which title or code it amended.
- Whether it created network-adequacy duties.
- Whether it imposed disclosure requirements.
- Which agency administers the provision.
- Whether implementing regulations are required.
- Whether the provision applies to all plans or only specified markets or products.

### Build a citation crosswalk

A crosswalk makes unsupported references visible:

```json
{
  "claimed_citation": "§28-725",
  "claimed_topic": "network adequacy",
  "citation_found": false,
  "possible_authority": [
    {
      "source": "Act 68 of 1998",
      "status": "requires verification",
      "relationship": "possible underlying insurance-law authority"
    },
    {
      "source": "Act 2 of 2023",
      "status": "requires verification",
      "relationship": "possible later amendment or disclosure authority"
    }
  ],
  "treatment": "Do not cite §28-725 as an operative code section"
}
```

### How to describe a failed citation search

Do not write:

> Sections §28-725 and §29-8719 do not exist.

That is stronger than the evidence unless the complete official code has been checked.

Use:

> The references “§28-725” and “§29-8719” could not be confirmed as valid, citable sections in the official sources reviewed. They are therefore excluded from the legal authority list pending source-level confirmation.

That wording distinguishes:

- “I did not find it,” from
- “It definitely does not exist.”

### Acceptance criteria

Step 4 is complete when:

- Act 68 of 1998 has been located in an official source.
- Act 2 of 2023 has been located in an official source.
- Their actual subject matter has been recorded.
- Relevant operative provisions have been quoted or pinpointed.
- The claimed section numbers have been tested against the appropriate code or session-law source.
- Invalid or unresolved references are excluded from final citations.
- Network adequacy and disclosure claims are tied to verified provisions, not merely related legislation.

A compact research-state file could be generated like this:

```bash

cat > research/authority/citation_crosswalk.json <<'EOF'
{
  "claims": [
    {
      "claim_id": "network-adequacy-001",
      "claim": "Puerto Rico law requires network adequacy disclosure",
      "authority": null,
      "status": "unverified",
      "allowed_in_final_report": false
    },
    {
      "claim_id": "citation-001",
      "claim": "§28-725 is the governing network-adequacy provision",
      "authority": "§28-725",
      "status": "not_confirmed",
      "allowed_in_final_report": false
    }
  ]
}
EOF
```

## Step 8 — Block unsupported citations from the Vob/Appeal/IDR templates

### Purpose

This is an implementation control. It prevents unsupported statutes from appearing in:

- Verification-of-benefits templates.
- Appeals.
- Internal dispute-resolution materials.
- Member letters.
- Provider communications.
- Compliance checklists.
- Automatically generated legal summaries.

The key rule is:

> A citation may be used in an output template only if it already exists in the verified `statutes_2026.json` data source.

The template must not invent citations, infer them from prose, or accept a citation merely because it resembles a valid statute.

### Define the allowed-citation rule

A citation is usable only when all of these are true:

```text
citation exists in statutes_2026.json
status is enacted or otherwise legally operative
jurisdiction matches the document
effective date covers the document date
source URL is present
verification flag is true
```

A stricter record might look like this:

```json
{
  "citation": "40 L.P.R.A. § 1234",
  "jurisdiction": "Puerto Rico",
  "status": "enacted",
  "effective_from": "2026-01-01",
  "effective_to": null,
  "source_url": "https://official-source.example/law",
  "verified": true,
  "allowed_templates": [
    "vob",
    "appeal",
    "idr"
  ]
}
```

An unverified citation should remain in research notes, but not in production output:

```json
{
  "citation": "§28-725",
  "status": "unconfirmed",
  "verified": false,
  "allowed_templates": []
}
```

### Add a hard validation gate

The generator should fail closed. If a citation is missing, it should not silently print it.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
import json
from pathlib import Path

STATUTES = Path("data/statutes_2026.json")

def load_statutes():
    with STATUTES.open(encoding="utf-8") as handle:
        data = json.load(handle)

    if isinstance(data, dict):
        return data.get("statutes", [])

    if isinstance(data, list):
        return data

    raise ValueError("statutes_2026.json must contain a list or a statutes property")

def verified_citations(statutes):
    allowed = set()

    for item in statutes:
        citation = item.get("citation")
        if not citation:
            continue

        if item.get("verified") is True and item.get("status") in {
            "enacted",
            "operative",
            "regulation"
        }:
            allowed.add(citation)

    return allowed

def validate_template_citations(template_citations, allowed):
    rejected = sorted(set(template_citations) - allowed)

    if rejected:
        raise SystemExit(
            "Blocked unsupported citations:\n- " + "\n- ".join(rejected)
        )

def main():
    statutes = load_statutes()
    allowed = verified_citations(statutes)

    requested = [
        "§28-725",
        "§29-8719"
    ]

    validate_template_citations(requested, allowed)
    print("All citations are verified and permitted.")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### Prefer citation IDs over free-form text

Instead of allowing templates to accept arbitrary strings:

```json
{
  "citation": "§28-725"
}
```

use a verified identifier:

```json
{
  "statute_id": "pr_act_68_1998_network_adequacy"
}
```

The renderer then retrieves the citation only from the verified data file. This prevents a user or upstream model from injecting an unverified citation directly into the document.

```python
def resolve_statute(statutes_by_id, statute_id):
    statute = statutes_by_id.get(statute_id)

    if not statute:
        raise KeyError(f"Unknown statute_id: {statute_id}")

    if not statute.get("verified"):
        raise ValueError(f"Unverified statute blocked: {statute_id}")

    return statute
```

### Add a visible fallback

When no verified authority exists, the output should say so rather than fabricate one:

```text
Legal authority: No verified statutory citation available.
Action: Route for manual legal or compliance review.
```

Do not allow the template to produce:

```text
Pursuant to §28-725...
```

unless that exact citation is present and verified in `statutes_2026.json`.

### Test cases

The gate should test at least these conditions:

- Verified citation is accepted.
- Unknown citation is rejected.
- Unverified citation is rejected.
- Repealed citation is rejected.
- Citation from the wrong jurisdiction is rejected.
- Citation not effective on the document date is rejected.
- Missing source URL is rejected.
- Duplicate citations are normalized.
- A template with no citations can still render with a manual-review notice.

### Acceptance criteria

Step 8 is complete when:

- All template citations come from `statutes_2026.json`.
- Unverified citations cause a hard failure.
- The system distinguishes enacted, proposed, repealed, and unresolved authorities.
- Each accepted citation has a source and verification metadata.
- The output records which citations were used.
- A missing citation produces a review queue rather than invented legal text.
- CI or a pre-generation test runs the citation validator automatically.

A practical pipeline is:

```bash

python3 scripts/validate_statutes.py
python3 scripts/validate_templates.py
```

The overall logic is:

```text
research source
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

```

In short: **Step 1 verifies whether the proposed measures became law, Step 4 verifies the actual legal authorities instead of trusting questionable section numbers, and Step 8 enforces those findings mechanically so unsupported citations cannot reach an end-user document.**
