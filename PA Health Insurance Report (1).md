## Abstract

This white paper examines proposed Plan Year 2027 health insurance rate filings submitted to the Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID), evaluating market dynamics, enrollment attrition, and implications for population health equity. For 2027, Pennsylvania health insurers requested enrollment-weighted average premium increases of 17.1% in the individual market and 11.5% in the small-group market. Individual market rate change requests ranged from 10.46% (Geisinger Health Plan) to 40.90% (Ambetter Health of Pennsylvania). A critical driver of Ambetter's request was a bronze-tier exit resulting in a 17.46% bronze-to-silver plan-mapping differential layered onto a 23.44% silver-to-silver rate hike. Concurrently, longitudinal data from Pennie (Pennsylvania’s state-based marketplace) demonstrated a 13.0% decline in active enrollment between February 1 and August 1, 2026 (falling from 486,000 to 422,869 enrollees). This attrition followed the end-of-2025 expiration of enhanced federal premium tax credits, which doubled net monthly premium burdens for average enrollees. Following federal litigation (*City of Columbus v. Kennedy*), Pennie restored the traditional 15-week open enrollment period (November 1, 2026–January 15, 2027). This paper highlights the public health risks of premium shock, coverage churn, and tier migration, offering policy recommendations to safeguard health equity and continuity of care.

*Keywords:* Health insurance rate filings, Pennie, ACA marketplace, premium tax credits, health equity, coverage churn, Pennsylvania public health.

## Executive Summary and Policy Context

In July 2026, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID) released proposed rate filings for Plan Year 2027. Insurers operating across the state requested an enrollment-weighted average premium increase of 17.1% in the individual market and 11.5% in the small-group market (Pennsylvania Insurance Department [PID], 2026). These proposed rates are subject to administrative review, public comment, and formal approval by the Insurance Commissioner before finalization in autumn 2026.

The proposed increases coincide with severe post-enrollment coverage attrition across Pennsylvania's state-based exchange, Pennie. Following the expiration of enhanced federal premium tax credits on December 31, 2025, individual marketplace enrollees experienced a mean net premium payment increase of approximately 102% (WVIA, 2026). Active Pennie enrollment subsequently contracted by 63,131 individuals between February 1 and August 1, 2026, signaling diminished premium affordability among working-class and moderate-income households.

Simultaneously, legal challenges regarding federal open enrollment duration limits culminated in a judicial stay (*City of Columbus v. Kennedy*, 2026), enabling Pennie to restore its 15-week open enrollment window (November 1, 2026–January 15, 2027) rather than enforcing a shortened 9-week period (CMS, 2026; Pennsylvania Office of Rural Health [PORH], 2026). This white paper contextualizes these statutory, financial, and market dynamics through a public health framework.

### Deconstructing Ambetter's Rate Filing Mechanics

The highest single individual market rate request—submitted by Ambetter Health of Pennsylvania at 40.90%—is driven by a structural plan tier exit rather than an across-the-board rate hike on existing benefit packages. PID's statutory review indicates that Ambetter's filing combines two distinct mathematical components:

1. **Silver-to-Silver Base Rate Increase:** A base premium adjustment of 23.44% applied to silver metal tier plans.
2. **Bronze-to-Silver Plan Mapping Differential:** A 17.46% premium differential resulting from Ambetter’s withdrawal from the bronze metal tier, which triggers automatic administrative mapping of existing bronze policyholders into silver tier plans (PID, 2026).

Consequently, while the reported top-line rate increase is 40.90%, existing silver enrollees face a 23.44% increase, whereas bronze policyholders mapped into higher-actuarial-value silver plans absorb the combined cost effect.

## Pennie Marketplace Attrition and Public Health Equity Implications

### Longitudinal Enrollment Contraction

Following the expiration of enhanced federal premium subsidies under the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) and Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) on December 31, 2025, Pennie observed systematic, month-over-month enrollment decay throughout 2026.

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |

| March 24, 2026 | 471,442 | -14,558 | -3.00% |
| May 1, 2026 | 452,000 | -34,000 | -7.00% |

*Note.* Data compiled from Pennie administrative dashboards (2026). Total attrition from Feb 1 to Aug 1 equals $\Delta = 63,131$ individuals, representing a relative contraction of approximately $13.0\%$.

As of August 2026, Pennie reported three distinct loss metrics:
* **85,000 Open Enrollment Non-Effectuations:** Individuals who selected a plan during open enrollment but failed to remit initial binder payments.
* **108,000 Post-Enrollment Terminations:** Active consumers who dropped coverage mid-year following the initial close of open enrollment.
* **>191,000 Cumulative Cancellations:** Aggregate terminations including both non-effectuations and mid-year plan drops.

### Public Health and Population Equity Analysis

The convergence of double-digit rate hikes (17.1% individual average) and post-subsidy price increases presents severe public health risks across Pennsylvania:

1. **Exacerbation of Health Disparities and Preventable Mortality:** The loss of 63,000+ active enrollees disproportionately affects lower-middle-income individuals who fall just above Medicaid eligibility thresholds. Uninsured individuals frequently forego routine care, leading to delayed diagnoses of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and oncological conditions.
2. **Coverage Churn and Treatment Interruption:** Forced plan reassignment—such as Ambetter’s transition of bronze enrollees to silver plans—disrupts provider network continuity. Patients undergoing active treatments (e.g., chemotherapy, mental health therapy, substance use recovery) face catastrophic administrative friction, re-authorization delays, or cost-sharing spikes.
3. **Underinsurance and Financial Vulnerability:** As silver premiums rise by $15\% \text{ to } 33\%$, low-income enrollees may downgrade to high-deductible plans or drop coverage entirely. Increased out-of-pocket exposure drives medical debt, prescription non-adherence, and emergency department overutilization.

## Open Enrollment 2027 Regulatory Timeline and Legal Framework

The administrative execution of Plan Year 2027 open enrollment was heavily shaped by federal rule-making and federal court intervention.

```
       [Spring 2026]              [July 16, 2026]              [Nov 1, 2026 – Jan 15, 2027]
   Pennie Board shortened       U.S. District Court stayed      Pennie restored 15-week OE window
   OE window to 9 weeks         CMS 9-week rule limit           Coverage effective Jan 1 / Feb 1
 (Oct 15 – Dec 15, 2026)      (City of Columbus v. Kennedy)     (Confirmed by CMS July 31)
```

In early 2026, Pennie's Board of Directors aligned state policy with the 2027 Notice of Benefit and Payment Parameters, shortening open enrollment to 9 weeks (October 15–December 15, 2026). However, on July 16, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the District of Maryland granted a nationwide stay in *City of Columbus v. Kennedy* (No. 26-2215). CMS subsequently issued formal administrative guidance on July 31, 2026, confirming that state exchanges could maintain traditional enrollment windows (CMS, 2026; PORH, 2026).

Consequently, Pennie reinstated the traditional 15-week schedule:
* **Open Enrollment Window:** November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027.
* **Coverage Start Dates:** January 1, 2027 (for plan selections completed by December deadline); February 1, 2027 (for selections completed between mid-December and January 15).

## Policy Recommendations for Public Health Action

To mitigate rate shock and maintain market stability, state policymakers and public health administrators should pursue four key interventions:

1. **Rigorous Insurance Department Rate Review:** PID should utilize its full statutory authority to scrutinize carrier administrative costs and medical loss ratios, rejecting unjustified increases that exacerbate plan drop-outs.
2. **Targeted Re-enrollment and Navigational Support:** State funding should prioritize outreach to the 85,000 non-effectuated consumers and former Ambetter bronze enrollees, ensuring smooth transition into alternative affordable plans.
3. **State-Level Premium Subsidies:** Pennsylvania should evaluate state-funded premium tax credits to fill the coverage gap left by expiring federal ARPA/IRA subsidies.
4. **Monitoring Network Adequacy:** PID must ensure that cross-tier plan mapping (e.g., bronze to silver) does not strip vulnerable enrollees of access to essential specialty care networks.
