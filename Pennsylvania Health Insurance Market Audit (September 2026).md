## Cross-Sectional Market Audit and Public Education White Paper for All Nine Rating Areas

**Publication Date:** September 6, 2026  
**Scope:** Pennsylvania Individual and Small-Group ACA-Compliant Health Insurance Markets, Pennie Coverage Dynamics, 2026 Enrollee Impacts, and Proposed 2027 Rate Filings  
**Audience:** Consumers, Healthcare Navigators, Community Organizations, Journalists, Policymakers, Legal-Aid Advocates, Employers, and Health Policy Stakeholders

## Executive Summary

As of September 2026, Pennsylvania’s individual and small-group health insurance markets are undergoing a critical affordability challenge. The convergence of federal policy shifts, rising healthcare utilization, escalating specialty prescription drug costs, and structural carrier rate requests has created widespread financial friction for households and small businesses across the Commonwealth.

The principal market drivers shaping September 2026 include:
1. **Expiration of Enhanced Federal Subsidies:** The expiration of Enhanced Premium Tax Credits (EPTCs)—originally authorized under the American Rescue Plan and extended by the Inflation Reduction Act—took effect for Plan Year 2026. Without these enhanced federal subsidies, net monthly premiums for affected Pennie enrollees increased by an average of 102%.
2. **Substantial 2026 Rate Hikes:** The Pennsylvania Insurance Department (PID) approved a weighted average individual market rate increase of 21.5% for 2026 (rejecting $50.1 million in excessive carrier requests). Small group plans saw an average increase of 13%.
3. **Severe Enrollment Contraction & Coverage Drops:** Peak Pennie enrollment stood at nearly 500,000 enrollees in 2025. Following the 2026 Open Enrollment Period (OEP), 85,000 enrollees dropped coverage due to cost. As of August 1, 2026, total effectuated enrollment on Pennie plummeted to 422,869—representing over 191,000 total coverage cancellations since late 2025.
4. **Impending 2027 Rate Requests:** Filings submitted by carriers for Plan Year 2027 propose an additional weighted average increase of **17.1% in the individual market** and **11.5% in the small group market**. Requests range from 10.46% (Geisinger) to 40.90% (Ambetter Health of PA).
5. **Structural Tier Shifts and "Underinsurance":** To mitigate monthly premium spikes, thousands of consumers migrated down from Silver to Bronze tier plans, accepting annual deductibles exceeding $7,000–$9,000. Additionally, major carriers (e.g., Ambetter) have proposed completely exiting the Bronze tier for 2027, forcing further plan re-assignments.

This white paper provides an unbiased, evidence-based audit of market mechanics across Pennsylvania's nine designated rating areas, offering actionable consumer guidance and a legally grounded advocacy framework for public oversight.

## 1. Market Conditions Entering September 2026

### 1.1 Summary of Approved 2026 Rate Increases
In late 2025, the Pennsylvania Insurance Department reviewed and finalized rate adjustments for the 2026 coverage year. The state-approved individual market average weighted increase was **21.5%**. Approved changes varied considerably among individual market carriers:

* **Ambetter Health of Pennsylvania:** +37.8%
* **Capital Advantage Assurance Company:** +24.6%
* **Keystone Health Plan Central:** +22.4%
* **Keystone Health Plan East:** +22.0%
* **Highmark Benefits Group:** +18.4%
* **Highmark Inc.:** +17.7%
* **Health Partners Plans (Jefferson Health Plans):** +16.7%
* **Highmark Coverage Advantage:** +14.5%
* **Geisinger Quality Options:** +13.8%
* **Geisinger Health Plan:** +11.6%

PID regulators noted that medical inflation, increased post-pandemic utilization of advanced outpatient procedures, expanding weight-loss and biologic therapy drug costs, and adverse selection following subsidy shifts necessitated these adjustments. PID rate reviews reduced total carrier-requested increases by approximately $50.1 million statewide.

### 1.2 Subsidy Expiration & Enrollment Disruption
The structural collapse of the EPTC "subsidy cliff" protection reinstated federal income eligibility caps at 400% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) and raised required household premium contributions across all brackets:

| Household Income (% FPL) | 2021–2025 Enhanced Cap (% Income) | 2026 Standard ACA Cap (% Income) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Below 150% FPL** | 0.0% | 2.10% – 4.19% |
| **150% – 200% FPL** | 0.0% – 2.0% | 4.19% – 6.60% |
| **200% – 250% FPL** | 2.0% – 4.0% | 6.60% – 8.44% |
| **250% – 300% FPL** | 4.0% – 6.0% | 8.44% – 9.96% |
| **300% – 400% FPL** | 6.0% – 8.5% | 9.96% |
| **Above 400% FPL** | 8.5% Cap | **No Subsidy (Full Retail Rate)** |

#### Impact Highlights:
* **The "Subsidy Cliff" Re-emerges:** An individual earning $63,840 or a family of four earning $132,000 (above 400% FPL) lost all Advance Premium Tax Credits (APTC), exposing them to full, unsubsidized retail rates averaging $800–$1,800+ per month depending on age and region.
* **Effectuated Drops:** Data published by the Independent Fiscal Office (IFO) and Pennie show effectuated enrollment dropped by 8% in mid-2026 compared to 2025. Over 191,000 individuals have canceled or lost Pennie coverage between Open Enrollment 2026 and August 2026.
* **State Affordability Program (Act 54 of 2024):** Pennsylvania established a state-level exchange affordability framework under Act 54. However, state funding allocations ($50 million targeted) replace only a fraction of the $600 million in lost annual federal EPTC subsidies.

### Rating Area 1: Northwest Pennsylvania
* **Counties:** Clarion, Crawford, Erie, Forest, McKean, Mercer, Venango, Warren.
* **Participating Individual Carriers (2026–2027):** Ambetter Health of PA, Highmark Inc., Highmark Coverage Advantage, UPMC Health Network, UPMC Health Plan.

#### Market Audit & Analysis:
Rating Area 1 is characterized by vast geographic distances and sparse rural populations surrounding the urban center of Erie. Healthcare access is dominated by two competing regional health systems: UPMC and Allegheny Health Network (AHN / Highmark).
* **Provider Network Dynamics:** Consumers selecting narrow-network plans (e.g., UPMC-only or Highmark-only products) face severe travel burdens if specialized clinical care is required outside their primary network. McKean and Warren counties face acute shortages of local sub-specialists and behavioral health clinicians.
* **2026–2027 Cost Strain:** Highmark requested a 16.16% increase for 2027, while UPMC requested 15.82%. Ambetter's proposed exit from the Bronze tier in 2027 will heavily impact lower-income rural residents in Mercer and Crawford counties who relied on Bronze plans as safety-net coverage.

### Rating Area 2: North Central Rural
* **Counties:** Cameron, Elk, Potter.
* **Participating Individual Carriers (2026–2027):** Ambetter Health of PA, Geisinger Health Plan, Geisinger Quality Options, Highmark Inc., UPMC Health Network.

#### Market Audit & Analysis:
Rating Area 2 represents the least densely populated rating area in Pennsylvania. 
* **Provider Network Dynamics:** Local hospital facilities are extremely sparse. Geisinger and UPMC hold significant market share, but emergency transport services and regional specialty access require long-distance travel to Centre or Lycoming counties.
* **2026–2027 Cost Strain:** Geisinger Health Plan requested a 10.46% increase for 2027—the lowest major request in the state—offering comparative stabilization for Geisinger-network enrollees. However, Highmark (16.16%) and UPMC (12.03%) rate hikes, coupled with the elimination of EPTCs, have driven higher disenrollment rates here than the statewide average.

* **Counties:** Bradford, Carbon, Clinton, Lackawanna, Luzerne, Lycoming, Monroe, Pike, Sullivan, Susquehanna, Tioga, Wayne, Wyoming.
* **Participating Individual Carriers (2026–2027):** Ambetter, Geisinger, Geisinger Quality Options, Health Partners Plans, Highmark Benefits Group, Oscar Health Plan, Partners Insurance Co., UPMC Health Network.

#### Market Audit & Analysis:
Encompassing Scranton, Wilkes-Barre, Williamsport, and the Pocono mountain corridor, Rating Area 3 reflects stark contrasts between urbanized centers and isolated mountain communities.

Network cross-border care into New York state (e.g., for Tioga and Susquehanna residents) creates complex prior-authorization barriers.
* **2026–2027 Cost Strain:** Multi-carrier presence creates superficial competition, but rate requests for 2027 remain high: Highmark Benefits Group requested +21.40%, Partners Insurance Co. +20.51%, and Oscar +15.39%. Dissolution of EPTCs caused thousands of middle-income households in Lackawanna and Luzerne counties to lose subsidy assistance entirely.

### Rating Area 4: Southwest / Pittsburgh Metropolitan
* **Counties:** Allegheny, Armstrong, Beaver, Butler, Fayette, Greene, Lawrence, Washington, Westmoreland.
* **Participating Individual Carriers (2026–2027):** Ambetter, Highmark Inc., Highmark Coverage Advantage, UPMC Health Network, UPMC Health Plan.

#### Market Audit & Analysis:
Rating Area 4 is Pennsylvania’s second-largest market by population and the historic battleground for carrier-provider vertical integration between UPMC and Highmark (AHN).

Misunderstanding these network boundaries remains the leading cause of out-of-network balance billing disputes in southwestern PA.
* **2026–2027 Cost Strain:** With Highmark requesting +16.16% and UPMC requesting +15.82% for 2027, households facing double-digit premium increases are increasingly forced to switch carriers, disrupting long-standing physician relationships across competing hospital networks.

### Finding 1: The "Underinsurance" Trap
The 2026 market shift caused an unprecedented migration from Silver plans (which offer Cost-Sharing Reductions for lower-income enrollees) to Bronze plans. A Bronze plan carries lower monthly premiums but imposes annual deductibles between **$7,000 and $9,200 for individuals** ($14,000–$18,400 for families). 
* **Clinical Consequences:** Insured individuals enrolled in high-deductible Bronze plans routinely defer necessary routine medical care, bloodwork, specialty evaluations, and diagnostic imaging due to immediate out-of-pocket costs, resulting in delayed diagnoses and emergency room dependency.

### Finding 2: Rural Disproportionate Burden
Data from Pennie and the Independent Fiscal Office confirm that rural counties in Rating Areas 1, 2, 3, and 5 experienced higher proportional disenrollment rates than urban counties. Lower median household incomes combined with single-carrier dominance in rural regions leave rural consumers with limited ability to switch plans to avoid rate spikes.

### Finding 3: Benchmark Plan Volatility
The ACA subsidy formula ties a consumer’s tax credit to the cost of the **second-lowest-cost Silver plan (SLCSP)** in their specific rating area. When benchmark plans shift year-over-year due to carrier repricing:
* Consumers who remain in their existing plan may experience massive net premium increases if their plan is no longer the benchmark.
* Navigators must actively educate enrollees that staying with the same plan without reviewing annual Pennie updates can cost thousands of dollars in lost subsidies.

### Finding 4: The 2027 Ambetter Bronze Exit Threat
Ambetter’s 2027 regulatory filing to completely eliminate Bronze plans across Pennsylvania creates an urgent administrative hurdle. Tens of thousands of Bronze plan holders will be automatically cross-walked to Ambetter Silver plans (costing 23.44% to 40.90% more) unless they proactively select a alternative Bronze plan from another carrier during the 2027 Open Enrollment Period.

#### Step 1: Calculate "Total Annual Exposure" (Not Just Premium)
Consumers must evaluate plans using the **Total Annual Cost Formula**:

$$\text{Total Annual Exposure} = (12 \times \text{Net Monthly Premium}) + \text{In-Network Annual Out-of-Pocket Maximum}$$

* *Example:* Plan A has a $200/month premium and a $8,500 Max Out-of-Pocket ($\text{Total Exposure} = \$2,400 + \$8,500 = \$10,900$). Plan B has a $350/month premium and a $3,000 Max Out-of-Pocket ($\text{Total Exposure} = \$4,200 + \$3,000 = \$7,200$). For a consumer expecting surgery or managing chronic illness, Plan B is cheaper overall despite the higher monthly premium.

#### Step 2: Verify Provider Networks & Formularies
* **Never assume doctor participation:** A health system may be "in-network" while individual attending physicians or medical groups are out-of-network.
* **Check Prescription Tiers:** Confirm whether required medications are Tier 1 (generic), Tier 2 (preferred brand), Tier 3 (non-preferred), or Tier 4 (specialty), and verify if prior authorization or step therapy applies.

#### Step 3: Income Reporting Vigilance
Because federal premium assistance reconciliation occurs at tax filing, Pennie enrollees must report any income increase or decrease within 30 days to prevent either:
1. Premium tax credit clawbacks on federal tax returns due to underreported income.
2. Unnecessary overpayment of monthly premiums due to unreported income reductions.

### 4.2 Guidance for Patients with Ongoing Medical Treatment
Patients undergoing active cancer treatment, scheduled surgeries, complex biologic therapy, or specialized mental health care must take specific protective steps before switching plans:

1. **Continuity of Care Protections:** Under Pennsylvania law and federal regulations, if a provider leaves a network or a patient switches plans, the patient may request a **Continuity of Care Transition Period** (typically 60 to 90 days) allowing them to continue treatment with their existing provider at in-network cost-sharing rates.
2. **Prior Authorization Re-Verification:** Prior authorizations **do not transfer** between insurance carriers. A pre-approval granted by Highmark in 2026 will not be honored by UPMC or Geisinger in 2027. New authorizations must be submitted by clinicians prior to January 1.
3. **Formulary Exceptions:** If a required maintenance drug is absent from a new plan’s formulary, the patient's physician must file an **Urgent Formulary Exception Request** demonstrating medical necessity before coverage takes effect.

---

### 4.3 Guidance for Small Employers
Small employers struggling with 11.5% average rate increases in the small group market have viable alternative coverage models:

* **Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangements (ICHRAs):** Employers can offer tax-advantaged reimbursements allowing employees to buy their own individual market plans on Pennie, tailoring plan choices to individual family medical needs while capping employer costs.
* **Qualified Small Employer HRAs (QSEHRAs):** Designed for businesses with under 50 full-time employees not offering group coverage, enabling tax-free reimbursement of medical expenses and individual premiums up to federal statutory limits.

## 5. Unbiased Legal Advocacy & Regulatory Oversight Framework

This framework details the statutory rights, administrative remedies, and oversight mechanisms available under Pennsylvania law to address rate excessiveness, network inadequacy, directory misrepresentations, and coverage denials.

### 5.1 Regulatory Rate Review Standards
Under the Pennsylvania Insurance Company Law and Act 159, insurance rates must not be **"excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory."**

#### Public Oversight Arguments for Rate Filings:
Advocates and public commentators evaluating 2027 carrier filings (such as Ambetter's +40.90% or Keystone Central's +33.83%) should submit formal public comments to the PID focusing on:
1. **Actuarial Assumptions vs. Realized Claims:** Evaluating whether carrier medical trend projections overestimate future utilization or prescription drug inflation compared to historical loss ratios.
2. **Administrative Expense Ratios & MLR Compliance:** Verifying that carriers comply with Medical Loss Ratio (MLR) mandates requiring at least 80% of individual market premium dollars to be spent on clinical care and quality improvement.
3. **Impact of Benefit Discontinuations:** Challenging whether forcing consumers off Bronze plans into higher-priced Silver tiers creates an unfair constructive rate increase that disproportionately harms lower-income populations.

---

### 5.2 Network Adequacy and Provider Directory Integrity

#### Pennsylvania Network Adequacy Mandates:
Under PID standards and 31 Pa. Code § 301.121, managed care plans must maintain network access ensuring that primary care, specialty care, emergency facilities, and pharmacy services are accessible within reasonable time and distance standards (e.g., primary care within 30 minutes or 20 miles in rural areas).

#### Directory Misrepresentation & "Ghost Networks":
When an insurer lists a specialist, hospital, or mental health clinician as "in-network" in an online directory, but the provider is no longer accepting new patients or is out-of-network, consumers face severe out-of-pocket harm.

## 6. Conclusion and Strategic Policy Recommendations

The September 2026 market audit demonstrates that Pennsylvania’s individual health insurance landscape is at a critical juncture. High baseline carrier rate requests, coupled with the loss of enhanced federal subsidies, threaten to erode a decade of progress in coverage expansion.

### Key Policy & Advocacy Recommendations:
1. **Legislative Funding of Act 54:** The Pennsylvania General Assembly should fully allocate funds for the State Health Insurance Exchange Affordability Program established under Act 54 of 2024 to provide direct state-level premium assistance for households earning between 200% and 400% FPL.
2. **Rigorous PID Scrutiny of 2027 Requests:** Regulators must critically review double-digit rate requests—specifically scrutinizing carrier product exits (such as Ambetter's Bronze exit)—to prevent excessive premium burden and constructive consumer dropouts.
3. **Enhanced Network Enforcement:** PID should initiate audit sweeps of carrier provider directories to eliminate "ghost networks" and enforce strict network adequacy standards in rural Rating Areas 1, 2, 3, and 5.
4. **Proactive Open Enrollment Outreach:** Navigators, legal aid organizations, and community groups must prepare for the Fall 2026 Open Enrollment period (November 1 – January 15) with aggressive educational outreach, ensuring consumers actively re-evaluate plan options rather than passively absorbing rate hikes or plan disenrollments.
