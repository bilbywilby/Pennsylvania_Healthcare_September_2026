# Wiki Page 3: Appeals, Legal Dispute Frameworks & Formulary Exceptions

## Overview & Legal Foundation

This page provides administrative templates, statutory frameworks, and
operational protocols for challenging insurance claim denials, illegal balance
billing practices, and prescription coverage restrictions. All dispute letters
and clinical appeals are structured to enforce contractual compliance under
Evidence of Coverage (EOC) agreements, Employee Retirement Income Security Act
(ERISA) standards, and statutory protections established by the No Surprises
Act (NSA).

## Section 1: Clinical Appeal Frameworks (Medical Necessity Denials)

When a pre-authorization or post-service claim is denied for lack of "Medical
Necessity," the plan member may initiate a formal administrative appeal
establishing contractual alignment with EOC definitions, supported by
diagnostic evidence from the treating physician.

- **Template 1.1** — `clinical_appeal_framework.md` — short-form standard appeal.
- **Template 1.2** — `formal_appeal_medical_necessity.md` — expanded, legal-grade
  Level 1 appeal with peer-review disclosure demands.

Both are provided as separate files (see below).

## Section 2: No Surprises Act (NSA) Balance Billing Disputes

Under the federal No Surprises Act (H.R. 133 — Consolidated Appropriations Act,
2021, Division BB, Title I — verified), patient cost-sharing for emergency care,
or for non-emergency services from out-of-network clinicians at in-network
facilities, is capped at the in-network Qualifying Payment Amount (QPA).
Out-of-network providers may not balance-bill beyond that in-network
cost-sharing threshold.

- **Template 2.1** — `nsa_balance_billing_dispute.md` — see below.

## Section 3: Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) Tier & Formulary Exceptions

Prescription formularies sort medications into cost-sharing tiers. When a
prescribed drug is non-preferred or excluded, a formal exception request can
seek a lower tier or coverage authorization.

## Section 4: Forensic Audit & Regulatory Benchmarks

- **Internal appeal filing window** — `VERIFIED`: ERISA (29 U.S.C. § 1133 / 29
  CFR § 2560.503-1) gives claimants a standard ~180 days from receipt of an
  adverse benefit determination to file an internal appeal.
- **External review (IMR) filing window** — corrected from the source draft,
  still needs plan-level confirmation: the federal external-review deadline
  under the ACA (45 CFR 147.136 / 29 CFR 2590.715-2719) is generally **4 months
  (120 days)** from the final internal denial, not 180 days. Pennsylvania's own
  external-review process may set its own window — confirm against the specific
  plan's EOC before citing a number in any published template.
- **EOB allowed-amount verification**: cross-check billed CPT/HCPCS codes
  against the EOB's contracted "Allowed Amount" to catch unbundling or
  fee-schedule inflation before payment.
- **Fourth-quarter timing**: if the YTD out-of-pocket maximum has been met,
  scheduling remaining diagnostics/therapies before the January 1 plan reset is
  standard consumer guidance, not a personalized recommendation.
