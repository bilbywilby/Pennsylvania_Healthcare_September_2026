# Pennsylvania Health Insurance Market Audit (2026 Plan Year)
*Revised March/April 2026 Technical Report & Market Evaluation*

---

## Executive Summary

The Pennsylvania health insurance market in 2026 is navigating a watershed moment defined by the decoupling of federal fiscal support from state-level actuarial realities. Following the expiration of the Enhanced Premium Tax Credits (EPTCs) established under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) at the conclusion of fiscal year 2025, the Commonwealth transitioned from a period of historic enrollment growth to a sharp, managed contraction driven by the federal "subsidy cliff."

The individual market, facilitated through the Commonwealth's state-based exchange (**Pennie**), concluded the 2026 Open Enrollment Period (OEP) with approximately $486,000$ enrollees, reflecting a $2.1\%$ year-over-year decline from the 2025 peak of $496,661$. By March 2026, active enrollment had contracted further to $471,289$. 

This aggregate figure masks intense underlying market volatility:
- **Net Premium Shock:** While gross approved individual premiums rose by a weighted average of $21.5\%$, the loss of enhanced federal tax credits produced an average net (after-subsidy) premium increase of $102\%$ for subsidized consumers.
- **Attrition Dynamics:** Approximately $85,000$ plan cancellations occurred during the 2026 Open Enrollment Period alone (nearly $1,000$ per day). Subsequent post-OEP attrition pushed cumulative disenrollments past $110,000$ by early Spring 2026.
- **Regulatory Intervention:** Pennsylvania Insurance Commissioner Michael Humphreys blocked $\$50.1\text{ million}$ in unjustified premium requests during the fall 2025 rate review cycle, paring down small-group requests to an average increase of $12.7\%$.

### 1. Rating Area 8: Philadelphia Metro
*Bucks, Chester, Delaware, Montgomery, Philadelphia*
- **Market Dynamic:** The densest and most actuarially complex market in Pennsylvania.
- **Subsidy Impact:** Philadelphia County recorded an average net premium increase of $214\%$ ($\approx +\$137\text{ PMPM}$), representing the highest urban subsidy shock in the state. Active enrollment dropped by $15.5\%$ (from $142,000$ down to $120,000$).
- **Network Alliances:** Independence Blue Cross (IBX) secured a new long-term agreement with the University of Pennsylvania Health System (Penn Medicine), stabilizing access for over $400,000$ regional members.
- **Pricing Dispersal:** While Keystone Health Plan East rose by $+22.04\%$, Partners Insurance Co. introduced a $-10.12\%$ rate decrease, creating significant cross-carrier plan switching opportunities.

### 2. Rating Area 4 & Area 1: Pittsburgh Metro & Western PA
*Allegheny, Armstrong, Beaver, Butler, Fayette, Erie, Crawford, etc.*
- **Carrier Rivalry:** Dominated by the ongoing rivalry between Highmark and UPMC. Highmark Inc. was approved for a $+17.75\%$ increase, whereas UPMC Health Plan received a $+24.78\%$ increase after the PID found evidence of higher risk-pool morbidity in UPMC’s individual filings.
- **Attrition Profile:** The region experienced a steep drop in young adult enrollees ($18\text{--}34$ demographic), exiting at $2.1\times$ the statewide baseline rate due to price sensitivity.

### 3. Rating Area 6: Lehigh Valley & Centre Region
*Lehigh, Northampton, Berks, Centre, Columbia, Montour, etc.*
- **Hospital Network Friction:** Marked by major provider-payer negotiations, including contract disruptions between Lehigh Valley Health Network (LVHN) and UnitedHealthcare. 
- **Cost Sharing:** Over $35\%$ of regional employers transitioned to High-Performance Networks (HPNs). Consumers face strict tiering between St. Luke’s University Health Network and LVHN facilities.

### 4. Rating Area 7 & Area 9: Capital & South Central Region
*Dauphin, Cumberland, Adams, Lancaster, York, Lebanon, Perry*
- **The "York County Premium Cliff":** Serves as the primary actuarial case study for EPTC expiration. State data demonstrates that a $60$-year-old married couple earning $\$82,000$ annually faced annual premium increases scaling from $\$7,032$ (in 2025 under EPTCs) to $\$35,712$ (in 2026 without EPTCs)—a $382\%$ increase.
- **Coverage Substitution:** High rates of migration were observed from the individual market into Medicaid or employer-sponsored dependent plans.

## Clinical & Structural Cost Drivers

### 1. GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Expenditure
The rapid growth in prescriptions for Glucagon-like Peptide-1 (GLP-1) drugs (e.g., Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound) for metabolic health and weight management has introduced severe underwriting friction:
- **Prior Authorization & Step Therapy:** Insurers across Pennsylvania have instituted mandatory clinical step-therapy protocols. Enrollees must document clinical failure on lower-cost generic alternatives before brand-name GLP-1 coverage is approved.
- **Formulary Exclusion:** Several carriers shifted anti-obesity medications (AOMs) to non-formulary status for individual plans in 2026 to curb PMPM drug trend escalation.

### 2. Provider Directory Integrity ("Ghost Networks")
A landmark statewide study conducted by Texas A&M University for the Pennsylvania Insurance Department surveyed nearly $7,000$ listed marketplace providers:
- **Inaccuracy Rate:** **$87\%$** of surveyed directory listings contained at least one major inaccuracy (e.g., wrong address, incorrect phone number, non-acceptance of plan, or inactive practice).
- **Enforcement Mandate:** Under the No Surprises Act and state regulatory guidelines, the PID is initiating "secret shopper" audits with administrative fines for non-compliant carriers.

### 3. 2026 Quantitative Network Adequacy Standards
Beginning in 2026, Pennie and the PID enforce strict appointment wait-time thresholds:
- **Routine Primary Care:** Wait time $\le 15$ business days.
- **Routine Behavioral Health:** Wait time $\le 10$ business days.

## Legislative & Regulatory Framework

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       STATE & FEDERAL POLICY IMPACTS                            |
|                                                                                 |
|  * Act 77 (PA): Expanded PID oversight over Pharmacy Benefit Managers (PBMs).   |
|  * 1332 Waiver: PA Reinsurance program yields ~4% premium relief statewide.     |
|  * One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBB): Stricter ACA eligibility verification.    |
|  * No Surprises Act: Binding Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR) protocols.     |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

1. **Pennsylvania Act 77:** Significantly expands state regulatory authority over Pharmacy Benefit Managers (PBMs). PBMs must submit annual network adequacy disclosures and drug rebate transparency reports to the Insurance Department.
2. **Pennsylvania 1332 Reinsurance Waiver:** Pennsylvania’s state reinsurance mechanism continues to mitigate baseline rates, providing an estimated $4\%$ gross premium reduction compared to what rates would be without state intervention.
3. **Federal Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR):** National IDR backlogs top $430,000$ unresolved claims, creating liquidity constraints for regional provider systems and indirect pressure on commercial premium rate-setting.

## Strategic Action Protocols

### For Navigators, Brokers, and Consumer Advocates
1. **Initiate Plan-Mapping Audits:** Actively re-screen all 2025 Silver-tier enrollees who migrated to Bronze plans in 2026. This group faces elevated risks of medical debt and mid-year non-payment plan terminations.
2. **Utilize Clinical Appeal Frameworks:** For denied diagnostic imaging, specialty pharmaceuticals, or elective outpatient procedures, leverage written clinical justifications referencing the treating physician's network tiering.
3. **Verify Provider Tiering:** Confirm that key regional health systems (e.g., Penn Medicine, Jefferson, UPMC, AHN, Geisinger, St. Luke's, LVHN) are designated as "Tier 1 In-Network" facilities prior to care delivery.

### For Health System & Carrier Policy Managers
1. **Address Risk Pool Deterioration:** Develop value-added primary care packages ($0\text{ dollars}$ copay virtual care, integrated behavioral health) to prevent further disenrollment of young, healthy enrollees.
2. **Directory Remediation:** Implement automated quarterly provider directory validation to ensure compliance with PID secret shopper audits and avoid federal No Surprises Act penalties.
